Understanding Pain & Your Recovery Journey

Explore our three core rehabilitation guides below. Click through each interactive concept to see how your body adapts, protects, and builds lasting resilience.

Pain Does Not Always Mean Damage

Understanding Pain & Recovery

1. Before Injury

Your body can tolerate a certain amount of physical load. Pain may warn you before that limit is reached.

2. After Injury

After an injury, your body may tolerate less load, and its protective system may become more sensitive.

3. During Rehabilitation

Gradual rehabilitation can help rebuild strength, function, and tolerance for activity. Pain sensitivity may change at a different pace.

Tissue Tolerance Pain Protection

Concept based on the Twin Peaks Model in Explain Pain Supercharged, by G. Lorimer Moseley and David S. Butler, Noigroup Publications (2017), Fig. 1.3.

Healing Isn’t Linear

Progress has ups and downs – keep going.

“A Setback is not the same as starting over.”

Notice how even during a temporary flare-up or dip, your overall function remains much higher than where you started. Trust the process. Keep moving forward.

Plateau of Latent Potential

Real change often happens before you can see it.

Sometimes progress isn’t obvious at first. Your body is working, building, and adapting — even when the changes aren’t visible yet. This is the plateau of latent potential.

The Ice Cube Example

A block of ice looks the same from 10°F to 31°F. You don’t see any change — but each degree brings it closer to the melting point.

At 32°F, it finally starts to melt.

Small, unseen changes add up until the breakthrough happens.

Drag slider to warm the room: Looks the same... but change is happening.

At 10°F: Solid ice. Your early exercises are laying the neural foundation.

The Bamboo Example

For the first few years, you may not see much growth above ground. But the bamboo is busy growing its roots underground.

After about 5 years of root development, it can grow up to 90 feet (27 meters) in just 6 weeks!

What you can’t see is still progress.
