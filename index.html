Understanding Pain & Your Recovery Journey

Explore our three core rehabilitation guides below. Click through each concept to discover how your body adapts, protects, and restores resilience over time.

Pain Does Not Always Mean Damage

Understanding Pain & Recovery

1. Before Injury

Your body can tolerate a certain amount of physical load. Pain may warn you before that limit is reached.

2. After Injury

After an injury, your body may tolerate less load, and its protective system may become more sensitive.

3. During Rehabilitation

Gradual rehabilitation can help rebuild strength, function, and tolerance for activity. Pain sensitivity may change at a different pace.

Tissue Tolerance Pain Protection

Concept based on the Twin Peaks Model in Explain Pain Supercharged, by G. Lorimer Moseley and David S. Butler, Noigroup Publications (2017).

Healing Isn’t Linear

Progress has ups and downs – keep going.

“A Setback is not the same as starting over.”

Notice how even during a normal flare-up, your overall baseline is significantly higher than day one. Trust the process. Keep moving forward.

Plateau of Latent Potential

Real change often happens before you can see it.

Sometimes progress isn’t immediately visible on the outside. Your neuromuscular system is actively recruiting, adapting, and remodeling underneath.

The Ice Cube Example

A block of ice looks unchanged from 10°F to 31°F. You don’t see movement, but each degree brings it closer to its melting point.

At 32°F, it finally transforms.

Small, unseen adaptations compound until the physical breakthrough occurs.

Drag temperature slider: Looks the same... but change is happening.

At 10°F: Solid ice. Early exercises establish neural recruitment and joint tolerance.

The Bamboo Example

For several years, little growth shows above ground as the plant anchors a profound root network.

Once the foundation is established, it can surge up to 90 feet in just 6 weeks!

What you can’t see is still essential progress.
